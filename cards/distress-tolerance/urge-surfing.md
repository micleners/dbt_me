Urges follow a wave: they build, peak, and fade. Most people act at the peak, believing it's unbearable — but an urge left un-acted-on loses strength on its own. Unlike distraction, you *stay with* the urge and observe it.

**5-minute practice:**

1. **Name the urge** — say or write "I have the urge to…". This creates space between impulse and response.
2. **Observe without judgment** — notice how it shows up in the body (tight chest, restless hands) with a scientist's curiosity; your job is to notice, not to stop it.
3. **Breathe and surf** — picture it as a wave; slow breathing keeps your balance as it crests and recedes.
4. **Remind yourself it will pass** — "I am not the urge. This is hard, but I've done hard things." Set a 2–5 minute timer and stay present; most urges start fading within it.

Afterward, reflect: did it pass, what helped, what would you try next time? See the source page for the ex-text worked example.
