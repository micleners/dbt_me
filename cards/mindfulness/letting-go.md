Building on noticing judgments (M9), this releases them by rewriting them factually. The simple act of observing a judgment already softens it; rewriting finishes the job. Practice alongside M9 daily for a week.

**Judgment into non-judgment.** Describe the situation as facts, acknowledge the feeling, note the thought — no judgmental words:
- *"I hate this baby crying on the plane. Not everyone has to be a parent!"* → *"I hear a baby crying in the seat behind. I feel angry at his parents and irritated."*
- *"My boyfriend is so selfish, I hate him!"* → *"My boyfriend forgot the plan we made today. He doesn't always do that. I feel angry and want to argue. I'm thinking he only thinks about himself (but that's not true)."*

**Repetitive judgments** (recurring self-criticism):
1. **Describe the judgment** objectively, stating only facts, as if from outside.
2. **How it makes you feel** — the emotional cost ("self-hatred... makes me want to smoke more").
3. **Life without the judgment** — how you'd feel and act differently, and what better outcome might follow.

Throughout the day, when the judgment arises, restate it non-judgmentally. Use openers like "I am thinking," "I have the feeling of." See the source page for the worksheet and full examples.
