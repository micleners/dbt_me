## Summary

The stress response tightens your muscles to get you ready to fight or flee. Deliberately tensing your muscles and then relaxing them can therefore reduce stress.

## Practice

1. Choose a muscle group in your feet and tense it for a few seconds while breathing in.
2. Silently say "Relax" and let go of all the tension in that group while breathing out.
3. Repeat muscle groups if you want, working from your feet up to your head. Focus on the relaxed feeling as much as you can.

**Optional sequence:** toes, calves, thighs, butt, pelvis, stomach, hands/forearms, upper arms, shoulders, neck, tongue/jaw, face, entire body.

## Notes

- T10 calls this P "Progressive muscle relaxation" and runs top to bottom (upper back and arms down through calves) with ~5-second holds and no breath pairing. The card's "Paired" version, the name in Linehan's manual, links tensing to the inhale and releasing to the exhale, and runs bottom to top. The opposite directions are a real discrepancy between sources.
- Integration idea: rename T10's last P to "Paired muscle relaxation" or note both names, citing the deck.
