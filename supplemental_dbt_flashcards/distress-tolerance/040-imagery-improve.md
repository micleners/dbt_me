## Summary

When life circumstances are distressing, visualization can serve three purposes: distraction, self-soothing, or strengthening hope and courage by bringing future goals and rewards more vividly to mind.

## Practice

- Picture yourself somewhere that makes you feel safe or happy. The place can be real or invented, drawn from childhood or a recent memory, or a blend of several places.
- Picture yourself coping effectively, as in the Cope Ahead skill (Card 94).
- Fantasize about your Life Worth Living, or about how it will feel once you reach a goal.
- Picture a loved one, ancestral or living, embracing you, holding your hand, or standing beside you.

## Notes

- The "coping effectively" suggestion maps directly to `emotion-regulation/coping-ahead` (Card 94), which uses visualization as rehearsal when calm rather than as relief during a crisis.
- The safe-place and loved-one images function like a mental version of `distress-tolerance/self-soothing`; the card names self-soothing as one of imagery's purposes.
- `mindfulness/thought-defusion` uses guided imagery for a different aim: distancing from thoughts, not replacing a painful moment.
