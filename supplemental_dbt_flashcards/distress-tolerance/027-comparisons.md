## Summary

When upset, focusing on the bad parts of your situation is normal, but it only intensifies painful emotions. Instead, compare your current situation to "worse" situations to help reduce distress.

## Practice

1. To evoke gratitude for your life (or some part of it), compare yourself to someone less fortunate than you. This can be someone you personally know, people you read about in the news, or people on reality TV shows or documentaries.
2. If comparing yourself to others makes you uncomfortable, compare yourself to yourself: think about past situations when you had more problems, fewer coping skills, or less social support than you do now.

## Notes

- The second "C" of ACCEPTS (`distress-tolerance/accepts`), which pairs comparison to a tougher past time with a caution against invalidating the pain.
- Step 2 is the lower-risk variant for anyone who finds other-comparison invalidating or shaming.
