# Problem Solving: DBT Emotion Regulation Exercise E9

## Overview

This exercise teaches a structured approach to managing triggering situations before they occur. The content explains that recognizing problematic situations enables proactive preparation, giving individuals a sense of control over emotionally overwhelming circumstances.

## Four-Step Problem-Solving Process

### Step One: Behavior Analysis

Identify the triggering event, primary emotion, and accompanying thoughts/feelings. The example provided describes a situation where critical comments trigger anger, shame, and negative self-talk about appearance.

### Step Two: What Can I Change?

Assess which elements are modifiable. As the guide notes, "Sometimes it's impossible to change the external event, but we can work on our thoughts and how we talk to ourselves internally during the situation." Select two aspects to address, prioritizing controllable factors like personal thoughts and responses.

### Step Three: Brainstorming Alternatives

Generate alternative thoughts or behaviors. The resource suggests reframing cognitive distortions, challenging generalizations, or changing behavioral responses. Examples include self-compassionate statements and assertive boundary-setting.

### Step Four: Put the Solution into Action

Implement chosen strategies when the situation arises again. The content acknowledges that consistent practice develops this as a learnable skill over time.
