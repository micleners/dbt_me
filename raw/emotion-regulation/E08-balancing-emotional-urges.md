# Balancing Emotional Urges: DBT Emotion Regulation Exercise

## Overview

This DBT exercise teaches individuals to act opposite to their natural emotional urges when those urges prove ineffective. As the page explains, "our emotions often naturally pair with certain behaviors," yet these automatic responses don't always serve us well.

## Four Key Emotions & Their Opposites

The exercise focuses on four emotions with high potential for problematic behavior:

| Emotion | Natural Urge | Opposite Action |
|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| Anger | Verbally or physically attack | Be polite, gentle, or nice |
| Anxiety/Fear | Avoid the anxiety-provoking situation | Remain in the anxiety-provoking situation |
| Sadness | Isolate from others | Seek support and socialize |
| Shame | Hide or avoid people | Approach others and share thoughts |

## Four-Step Process

**Step One:** Identify the opposite urges for each emotion.

**Step Two:** Recognize your emotion and evaluate whether your automatic urge is actually effective in the situation.

**Step Three:** Deliberately engage in the opposite behavior, recognizing that "what our feelings tell us is not always right, they are temporary and programmed mechanisms."

**Step Four:** Persist with the new behavior; meaningful emotional shifts require repetition and time.

## Example Application

The page provides a workplace scenario: someone experiencing shame about presenting prepared work considers calling in sick. By acting opposite to this avoidance urge and delivering the presentation anyway, they experience relief and accomplishment afterward.
