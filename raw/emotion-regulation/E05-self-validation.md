# Self Validation: DBT Skills Guide

## Overview

Self-validation is a core DBT emotion regulation technique designed to help individuals accept their emotional experiences rather than judge them. According to the resource, people who struggle with emotional regulation often compound their difficulties by adding "negative secondary emotions as a response" to their primary feelings.

## Three-Step Framework

The skill breaks down into three distinct phases:

### Step One: Acknowledging

Simply name the emotion without judgment. Rather than catastrophizing ("I'm always sad and weak"), use neutral language like "I am feeling sad."

### Step Two: Allowing

Accept that experiencing emotions is valid. The guide suggests statements such as: "It is okay to feel the way I do right now" and "This emotion is uncomfortable, but it won't hurt me."

### Step Three: Understanding

Examine the contextual factors that created the emotion. Focus on objective facts rather than self-criticism.

## Practical Application

The resource provides this example progression:
- Acknowledging: "Right now I feel frustrated with myself"
- Allowing: "This feels uncomfortable, but right now it is what it is"
- Understanding: Identifying specific circumstances (loneliness, depression) without self-judgment
