# E6: Myths About Emotions - DBT Emotion Regulation Exercise

## Overview

This dialectical behavior therapy resource addresses false beliefs people hold about emotions. The page explains that society often sends problematic messages about emotional expression, and this exercise helps identify and challenge common emotional myths.

## Eight Core Myths and Opposing Views

1. **Myth**: Showing feelings indicates weakness
   - **Opposing view**: Emotional expression is normal and reflects healthy reactions to life events

2. **Myth**: Emotions lack value or purpose
   - **Opposing view**: Emotions provide rapid feedback about our attitudes and serve important functions

3. **Myth**: Feelings always represent truth
   - **Opposing view**: Emotions can linger and distort reality; they should inform but not solely determine decisions

4. **Myth**: Acting on urges represents sound decision-making
   - **Opposing view**: Intense emotions can trigger harmful behaviors; developing coping skills improves outcomes

5. **Myth**: Changing emotions feels inauthentic
   - **Opposing view**: Accepting emotions naturally creates transformation without forced suppression

6. **Myth**: Everyone should feel identically in similar situations
   - **Opposing view**: Emotional responses vary among individuals and represent normal human diversity

7. **Myth**: Drama enriches life experience
   - **Opposing view**: Healthy emotional awareness provides psychological depth without destructive impulses

8. **Myth**: Impulsivity increases productivity
   - **Opposing view**: Respectful assertiveness and proactive planning generate better long-term results

## Three-Step Exercise Process

**Step One**: Review the myths and consider which ones resonate personally

**Step Two**: Rate each myth on a 1-10 scale regarding its prevalence in your thinking

**Step Three**: Examine how your top three myths affect your behavior and imagine alternative outcomes
