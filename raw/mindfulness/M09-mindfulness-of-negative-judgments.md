# M9: Negative Judgments - DBT Mindfulness Exercise

## Introduction

We make judgments constantly throughout our daily lives. These negative judgments are often unconscious and unproductive, stemming from cognitive biases and distorted thinking patterns. Such biases cause us to perceive situations, others, or ourselves in negative ways. As noted in the material, "Negative judgments often make us feel bad and make us less effective at managing the situation."

This mindfulness exercise helps you become more aware of your automatic negative thoughts. The subsequent exercise will focus on letting go of these judgments.

---

## Instructions

The purpose of this [mindfulness exercise](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/) is to become more aware of how unwanted negative judgements are becoming part of your automatic thoughts.

**Print the worksheet provided below before beginning.**

Over the course of one week, note when you have negative judgments by tracking these four elements:

### 1. Situation

Briefly describe what triggered your negative judgment. Document where you were and the time of day. Example: sitting in your office doing a dull task, or out with a friend drinking coffee.

### 2. Judgment

Write down the exact words that crossed your mind. Example: "I hate my monotonous job, I wish I was working on something more exciting and not stuck in an office."

### 3. Resulting Emotion

Document how you felt when making the judgment. Negative judgments typically produce negative emotions. Example: "I feel sad and depressed" or "I feel angry and annoyed."

### 4. Outcome

Describe what you did after the situation. Did you achieve your intended goal, or were negative emotions preventing effective action? Example: You got quiet and performed your job less effectively, or said something snide to your friend and ignored them.

**Complete this daily for one week, then proceed to the next exercise on letting go of negative judgments.**

## Example Entries

| Situation | Judgment | Emotions | Outcome |
|-----------|----------|----------|---------|
| Reading a book at home with loud noise in the house | "My family is so inconsiderate! They are being so selfish!" | Anger, feeling irritated | I stopped reading, had an argument with my family, got even angrier |
| Taking a walk in the park alone | "Of course I am walking by myself, nobody wants to hang out with me." | Sadness, feeling depressed and lonely | I didn't have a good time, went home and ate a lot |
| Watching a movie with girlfriend who laughs loudly | "Why does she always have to laugh so loudly! It's so embarrassing." | Feeling annoyed and embarrassed | I made a snarky comment, and we had an argument |
| Reading about a friend who became famous | "Ugh, he wasn't even that smart, how did he become so successful!" | Feeling envious and insecure | I got depressed, and I didn't feel like doing anything |
| Boss asked me to prepare budgets for next year | "This is so boring and is going to take forever." | Frustration, dread, feeling depressed | I didn't say anything to her—but now I resent her. I procrastinated on doing the work |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What if I can't find enough time during the day to do this exercise?**

Although tracking throughout the entire day is ideal, you may complete this exercise at day's end. During the day, practice mindfulness of situations triggering negative judgments to improve recall when documenting.

**Do negative judgments always cause negative emotions?**

Some negative judgments don't necessarily cause emotional pain (for example, "the weather is cold today"). This exercise focuses on negative judgments causing unproductive emotions.

**Should I completely try to avoid judging things?**

The goal isn't eliminating all judgments—some are necessary for assessing safety and performance. However, negative judgments are often unproductive. Rather than saying "This job is really bad for me," more specific thinking (environment stress, unfulfilling work) produces better outcomes.
