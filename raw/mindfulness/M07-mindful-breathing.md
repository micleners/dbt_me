# M7: Mindful Breathing

## Introduction

Breathing is something we do constantly without conscious thought, yet it sustains life. The Mindful Breathing exercise is a powerful, grounding technique that requires minimal resources and can be practiced anywhere. As the page explains, "If you feel anxious or if you are starting to panic, you breathe." This foundational practice helps redirect attention when the mind becomes clouded with unwanted thoughts.

The exercise offers three distinct approaches so practitioners can discover which resonates most effectively. The recommendation is to practice one version daily for a week.

**DBT Mindfulness principle:** "Mindful Breathing lets you calm your mind when you are losing control."

---

## Counting Breaths

**What is Counting Breaths?**

This technique uses numbers to maintain focus and attention on breath, providing structure to mindfulness meditation practice.

**How to Practice:**

1. Find a comfortable, quiet space where you won't be interrupted for 10 minutes
2. Sit comfortably and notice your current mood
3. Set a timer for 10 minutes
4. Count breaths from 1 to 5 on each exhale: "One... two... three... four... five"
5. Repeat this pattern without attempting to change your natural breathing rhythm
6. When your mind wanders (which is normal), gently redirect attention back to counting without self-judgment
7. After completing 10 minutes, observe whether your mood has shifted
8. Record your experience in the provided worksheet

---

## Sensations of Breathing

**What is Sensations of Breathing?**

This approach directs attention to physical sensations accompanying breath, cultivating present-moment awareness and relaxation.

**How to Practice:**

1. Select a comfortable, quiet location for 10 minutes of uninterrupted practice
2. Notice and describe your current mood
3. Set your timer for 10 minutes
4. Breathe normally while gradually becoming aware of your breathing
5. Focus on the sensation of air entering your nostrils
6. Pay attention to how this physical sensation feels
7. When attention drifts, gently return focus to breathing sensations without judgment
8. Reflect on whether you've previously noticed this tactile experience
9. After 10 minutes, evaluate any mood changes
10. Document your experience in the worksheet

---

## Diaphramatic Breathing

**What is Diaphragmatic Breathing?**

Also called belly breathing or deep breathing, this technique engages the diaphragm to promote deep, slow, and efficient respiration patterns.

**How to Practice:**

1. Find a comfortable space for 10 minutes of uninterrupted practice
2. Become aware of your baseline mood (rested, tired, excited, relaxed, anxious, etc.)
3. Set your timer for 10 minutes
4. Place one hand on your belly
5. Breathe in through your nose, feeling your hand move upward as your belly expands
6. As you exhale, relax your diaphragm and feel your hand move downward
7. When your mind becomes distracted, gently and without judgment return awareness to the belly movement
8. Maintain a steady, relaxed breathing rhythm
9. After 10 minutes, assess any changes in how you feel
10. Note your experience in the provided worksheet

## FAQs

**Q: This exercise seems boring to me. How can I find it more interesting?**

Approach the exercise as a practical tool for calming your mind and body. The perception of boredom is a judgment that may prevent you from experiencing its benefits. Consider remaining curious about outcomes—you may be pleasantly surprised.

**Q: The techniques seem so simple. How are they going to help me?**

These methods help redirect attention when experiencing negative emotions or thoughts. "The exercises activate the relaxation response in the body, leading to a reduction in stress levels." They calm the nervous system, lower heart rate, and decrease cortisol, promoting overall well-being.

**Q: I find this exercise very helpful and relaxing. Can I do it more than once a day?**

Yes. If you find consistent benefits, practicing multiple times daily is encouraged. Once mastered, the exercise can be done anywhere—waiting for transportation, in bed, during work breaks—providing immediate stress relief benefits. It's particularly useful for managing worry and anxiety tendencies.
