# M3: Internal vs. External Events

## Introduction

This DBT mindfulness exercise addresses the balance between internal and external awareness. Being overly focused internally—on thoughts, emotions, and physical sensations—can amplify psychological distress. Conversely, excessive external focus may lead to ignoring emotions, worsening emotional struggles.

The goal is to develop balanced awareness of both internal and external events to better regulate emotions daily.

## Instructions

### Step 1: Determine Your Orientation

Read the statements below and check those that apply:

**Internal Focus (top group):**
- My thoughts
- Emotions
- Physical sensations
- Urges
- Images, memories

**External Focus (bottom group):**
- What I see around me
- Different smells
- Sounds around me
- People around me
- The environment around me

If you checked mostly internal items, proceed to **PART A**. If mostly external items, proceed to **PART B**.

### Step 2: Practice Awareness

#### PART A: Bringing Awareness to External Events

Spend approximately 10 minutes total.

1. **Spatial Awareness**: Sit comfortably, then slowly stand. Notice the room's size and how much space your body occupies. Move slowly while breathing deeply, focusing on spatial dimensions. Sit again and continue.

2. **Visual Attention**: Observe lighting (natural or artificial), colors, size, and objects. Scan front, sides, back, up, and down. Gently redirect attention if distracted.

3. **Auditory Focus**: Notice room quietness, distant sounds, loud versus quiet sounds, near and far noises.

4. **Olfactory Awareness**: Detect any scents—pleasant or unpleasant—from detergent, food, coffee, or environmental sources.

5. **Integration**: Combine all sensations without prioritizing any single sense.

**Reflection**: Record your experience in writing, noting observations about sight, sound, smell, and your post-exercise feelings.

#### PART B: Bringing Awareness to Internal Events

Spend approximately 10 minutes total.

1. **Thought Observation**: Notice thoughts entering and leaving your mind without attachment or reactivity. Observe thought pace and quality without judgment.

2. **Emotional Awareness**: Identify your underlying mood and any strong emotions (contentment, anxiety, sadness). Note physical aspects of emotions.

3. **Physical Sensations and Urges**: Scan your body for comfort, tension, fatigue, or physical needs. Observe sensations with acceptance.

4. **Reflection**: Record thoughts, emotions, physical sensations, and post-exercise feelings.
