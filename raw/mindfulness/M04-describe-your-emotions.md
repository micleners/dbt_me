# M4: Describe Your Emotions

## Introduction

This mindfulness exercise helps clarify emotions that may feel unclear or overwhelming. By practicing regularly, you develop greater ability to identify your feelings and their underlying causes, leading to better emotional control and more effective responses to challenging situations.

The exercise addresses a common experience: feeling uneasy without understanding why, then attempting to suppress the emotion only to have it intensify physically and mentally. Learning to describe your emotions specifically enables you to understand what triggers them and how to manage them effectively.

## Instructions and Procedure

Spend at least 15 minutes on this exercise. Practice daily for a week, then use it as needed thereafter.

### Step One: Pick An Emotion

Select either a positive or negative emotion you're currently experiencing, or one you've felt recently. Choose a specific feeling rather than a vague state. Avoid selecting an emotion that feels overwhelmingly negative.

**Positive Emotions:** Energetic, Hopeful, Lively, Blissful, Joyful, Curious, Bubbly, Loved, Loving, Excited, Respected, Strong, Relieved, Delighted, Secure, Pleased, Happy, Interested, Satisfied, Determined, Smart, Proud, Content

**Negative Emotions:** Hurt, Nervous, Upset, Frustrated, Lonely, Restless, Depressed, Hopeless, Envious, Bored, Scared, Angry, Regretful, Shy, Empty, Sad, Indifferent, Irritated, Embarrassed, Enraged, Exhausted, Guilty, Insecure, Jealous

Write down your chosen emotion.

### Step 2: Draw a Picture of Your Emotion

Create a visual representation of what your emotion resembles. This abstract exercise helps assign concrete meaning to abstract feelings. Don't worry about artistic skill—what matters is that the image makes sense to you personally. For example, anger might be represented as a thunderbolt.

### Step 3: Write an Action Suitable For Your Emotion

Describe an action that naturally aligns with your emotion. If you feel depressed, the action might be withdrawing alone. If curious, it might be exploring a hobby or interest.

### Step 4: Describe the Intensity of the Emotion

Rate the emotion's intensity on a 0-10 scale, where 10 represents maximum intensity. Alternatively, use creative descriptions: "as hot as boiling water" or "as cold as ice."

### Step 5: Write Your Thoughts Arising From the Emotion

Document thoughts resulting from your emotion. Avoid using emotion words from the lists above—distinguish between emotions and the cognitions they generate. For example, feeling proud might produce the thought "I want to share this achievement with friends."

## Example

**Emotion:** Irritated

**Picture:** (Visual representation)

**Suitable Action:** Complaining about everyday chores

**Intensity:** 5 out of 10

**Thoughts:** "On top of my work responsibilities, I have the same household chores every day! Over and over again."

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is this exercise going to help me feel better and be more effective?**

Once you identify your emotions and recognize how you experience them, you gain greater control over your reactions. Additionally, by understanding what triggers negative emotions, you can reduce or eliminate those situations, helping you feel more in control of your life.

**Do these simple steps really work?**

Despite their apparent simplicity, these instructions provide an effective framework for exploring emotions deeply.

**What if I struggle with the exercise?**

Follow the instructions and do your best. Everyone completes this exercise differently. The creative elements may only make sense to you personally, and that's acceptable. With continued practice, you'll develop deeper emotional insights and growing confidence.
