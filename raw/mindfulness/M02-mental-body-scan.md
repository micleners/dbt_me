# Mental Body Scan: DBT Mindfulness Exercise

## Introduction

The Mental Body Scan, also called Body Scan Meditation, is a mindfulness practice designed to help you:

- Achieve muscle relaxation and stress relief
- Reconnect with your body to recognize its needs
- Increase calmness and feel more refreshed and relaxed

## Preparation

Before beginning:

- Select a quiet, interruption-free location
- Sit comfortably in a chair with feet flat on the floor
- Close your eyes
- Take several deep breaths

**Total Duration: 15 minutes** (Repeat daily for one week)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Lower Body Awareness (5 minutes)

Begin by focusing on your feet's contact with the floor. Notice sensations in your toes, soles, and ankles. Assess whether your feet feel tired or rested.

Move awareness upward to your lower legs. Observe any energized or heavy sensations. Progress to your knees, noticing how they contact the chair.

Continue scanning your thighs, pelvic area, and buttocks. Feel how your body connects with the chair surface.

**If your mind wanders:** Gently redirect attention back to the body without judgment.

### Step 2: Upper Body Awareness (5 minutes)

Begin with your stomach. Notice if it feels calm or nervous. Observe any sensations, including hunger.

Progress to your lower back, noting tension or relaxation. Breathe into any discomfort without resistance.

Scan your upper back and torso, feeling how these areas contact the chair. Move attention to your hands—fingers, palms, and forearms. Notice sensations where hands rest.

Continue through elbows and upper arms.

**If distracted:** Softly return focus to physical sensations.

### Step 3: Shoulders, Neck, and Head (5 minutes)

Direct attention to shoulders and neck muscles, common areas for stress-related tension. Note any tightness or pain.

Move focus upward to your head. Scan your chin, mouth, nose, eyes, and ears. Notice any tension, headaches, or relaxation.

Observe overall head sensations without judgment.

### Step 4: Document Your Experience

Use the provided outline to record:

- Areas with most notable physical sensations
- Parts that felt most tense
- Parts that felt most relaxed

Completing this daily for a week may reveal recurring tension patterns in specific body areas.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: The exercise seems too difficult. I can't do it.**

A: Start with what you can manage. Partial completion is acceptable; gradually extend your practice with each attempt.

**Q: My mind keeps wandering with many thoughts.**

A: Mind wandering is normal. Accept thoughts without judgment and redirect focus back to body sensations.

**Q: I noticed tension but couldn't fully relax it.**

A: This exercise develops body awareness and identifies discomfort rather than eliminating pain. Recognition enables better management.
