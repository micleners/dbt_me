# M8: Wise Mind - DBT Skills Exercise

## Introduction

"Wise Mind" is a Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) concept developed by psychologist Marsha M. Linehan. According to the resource, it "lies between the emotional mind (decision making and judging based entirely on our emotions, or the way we feel) and the reasonable mind (thoughts, decisions and judgments based entirely on facts and rational thinking)."

In wise mind practice, individuals acknowledge their emotions while also considering facts, logic, and long-term goals. This represents optimal functioning and can be cultivated through mindfulness practice.

## Instructions

### Step One: Find a Comfortable Space

Create an uninterrupted environment where you can focus on developing this skill. This exercise requires dedicated practice time and a calm setting.

### Step Two: Thinking About a Problem in Your Life

1. Begin with abdominal breathing to settle your mind. Place your arm on your belly and observe your breath moving in and out.

2. After several breaths, identify a problem that has been bothering you—whether it's career uncertainty, relationship concerns, or feelings of monotony.

3. Spend a couple of minutes gently observing what primary issue emerges in your mind.

### Step Three: Using Your Wise Mind (5-Minute Practice)

Follow these steps to access wise mind guidance:

**a) Gather Information**
Seek out facts, data, and reliable opinions. Examine logical aspects, weigh pros and cons, and consider potential consequences.

**b) [Identify Your Emotions](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/describe-your-emotions/)**
Acknowledge and name your emotions without judgment. Notice strong feelings, fears, or gut instincts that arise.

**c) Reflect On Values And Goals**
Consider your values, priorities, and long-term aspirations. Assess how different options align with these aspects.

**d) Mindful Awareness**
Engage in [mindfulness practices](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/) to cultivate present-moment awareness. Stay open and curious about your internal state.

**e) Integrate Rational And Emotional Factors**
Combine the information gathered with emotional awareness. Ask yourself: What does logic suggest? What are my emotions telling me? How do these align or conflict?

**f) Take Time for Reflection**
Avoid rushing decisions. Step away if needed to process information fully and return with fresh perspective.

**g) Embrace Flexibility**
Recognize that decision-making evolves. Remain open to adjusting your course if new information emerges or outcomes don't align with goals.

## Practice Recommendations

Complete this 10-minute exercise three times weekly. Consistency develops skill proficiency over time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I verify my answer comes from wise mind rather than emotional or reasonable mind?**

Confirm you were mindful of both emotions and objective facts. If emotions clouded your judgment without considering reality, the answer likely isn't from wise mind—consider cooling down before deciding. Conversely, if you blocked emotions entirely and ignored your body's reactions, you weren't integrating both necessary components.

**The exercise feels too abstract. Will I be able to do it?**

While more advanced than earlier mindfulness exercises, this skill develops through practice. Since you've already practiced emotional and thought mindfulness, wise mind combines these capabilities. If the first attempt doesn't yield an answer, try again later—many people need several sessions to become comfortable. Alternatively, revisit earlier [mindfulness exercises](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/) before returning to this one.

**My wise mind isn't producing a solution.**

This skill requires practice before becoming intuitive. Some people develop it naturally; others need more repetition. Additionally, you might not have fully accepted the problem or may harbor skepticism about the concept. Acknowledge these feelings and retry the exercise later. Stepping away temporarily allows cognitive processing. Consulting a trusted person for perspective can help, but remember that ultimate decisions rest with you.
