# M6: Thought Defusion

## Introduction

This exercise builds on the previous [Mental Noting](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/mental-noting/) practice.

Thought defusion (also called cognitive defusion) derives from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT). The core objective involves establishing psychological distance between yourself and your thoughts. When individuals become overly attached to negative thoughts, they often experience unnecessary mental and emotional suffering. Creating this separation can reduce discomfort and increase your ability to tolerate difficult thoughts.

**Key Benefits:** Regular practice of thought defusion has been shown to diminish how believable negative thoughts seem, increase comfort with having unwanted thoughts, and improve overall mood.

The exercise offers three versions of guided imagery, each designed to help you notice negative thoughts and then create space from them. Practice 2-3 times weekly, or whenever needed.

---

## Words in the Sand

**Duration:** 10 minutes

1. Close your eyes and imagine a vast desert with sand dunes stretching across the horizon
2. Calm your mind and become aware of your thoughts
3. Notice whether your thoughts are neutral or negative—observe without judgment
4. Imagine your thoughts written across the sand
5. Visualize wind gradually blowing the words away as you release each thought
6. Watch your mind become calmer and clearer as thoughts dissolve
7. Recognize that thoughts are mental products that need not have power over you

**Example Process:**

If you think *"why did I say something so stupid,"* progressively create distance:

- First: "I'm noticing a thought that what I said was stupid"
- Second: "I'm noticing I'm having a thought that what I said was stupid"
- Third: "I'm noticing I'm having just another thought about being stupid"
- Fourth: "I'm noticing I'm having another judgment about myself. This judgment is not a fact"

Then imagine this final statement written in sand, and as you exhale, visualize the wind erasing it completely. Continue with subsequent thoughts.

## Fish in the Ocean

**Duration:** 10 minutes

1. Close your eyes and imagine a beautiful ocean floor
2. Visualize sun rays penetrating the water and reaching the sandy bottom
3. Notice the crystal-clear water and friendly fish surrounding you
4. Become aware of your current thoughts—everyday concerns or larger problems
5. Imagine the first fish carrying away your first thought
6. Watch the fish swim away peacefully, leaving the serene ocean floor visible again
7. Repeat this process with each subsequent thought
8. Reflect on how you feel at the exercise's end

**Example:** If you're worried about low energy levels, mentally label this thought, imagine a fish carrying it away, then proceed with your next thoughts.

For detailed thought-distancing steps, refer to the "Words in the Sand" example above.

---

## Clouds in the Sky

**Duration:** 10 minutes

1. Close your eyes and visualize a windy sky with moving clouds
2. Notice the blue sky and its fresh appearance
3. Take a deep breath and focus on your thinking mind
4. Notice your thoughts without judgment or analysis
5. Imagine each thought written on individual clouds using one or two words
6. Visualize the wind carrying each cloud away as your thought departs
7. Continue watching thoughts appear on clouds that float away
8. Reflect on any changes in how you feel

**Example:** Your first cloud displays *"did I lock the door?"* Imagine the clouds carrying away this worry. Continue this process with each new thought that arises.

For detailed thought-distancing steps, refer to the "Words in the Sand" example above.

## FAQs

**Q: I struggle to visualize with my eyes closed. Is this exercise too difficult?**

A: Imaginative exercises feel unusual initially but become natural with practice. Approach this as a relaxing, enjoyable experience. Experiment with the different scenarios and settings.

**Q: Doesn't imagining thoughts disappearing mean I'm ignoring my problems?**

A: Research indicates over 80% of people regularly experience negative thoughts. This practice doesn't mean avoiding problems; instead, creating psychological distance allows your mind to find new solutions. Ten minutes of mindfulness and thought defusion provides mental rest and builds awareness to approach daily life differently.

**Q: What if I don't visualize the scenario exactly as described?**

A: Follow your natural imaginative tendencies. The instructions exist to facilitate the exercise. Feel free to modify elements—imagine a sunnier sky, a beach instead of desert, or other variations. The goal is practicing noticing and defusing your thoughts.
