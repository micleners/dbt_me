# Trust in Relationships

Building healthy relationships requires trustworthiness and surrounding yourself with dependable people. Many individuals have experienced negative interpersonal situations affecting their ability to trust others or be trustworthy themselves. This exercise focuses on three key components for building trust: honesty, understanding and respect, and consistency.

## Step One: Being Honest

Evaluate your typical honesty in relationships. Consider whether you feel comfortable sharing the truth and if you trust people around you enough to share your authentic thoughts and feelings, particularly when the truth feels difficult or uncomfortable.

First assess whether the person has earned your trust before sharing vulnerable information.

Identify a recent situation where sharing your honest opinion, thoughts, or feelings proved challenging. Practice formulating what you would say, despite discomfort.

Example: Rachel struggled telling her boyfriend she dislikes hanging out with his friends due to their insensitive jokes. Instead of being truthful, she claimed tiredness. A more honest approach: "I trust you'll understand. I feel offended and hurt by how your friends' jokes affect me."

## Step Two: Being Understanding and Respectful

Help people feel validated, appreciated, and heard during communication. Practice understanding by reflecting their viewpoint and emotions without judgment.

Others struggle to be honest when expecting criticism or judgment. Everyone acts based on reasons and unmet needs. While you needn't approve of their choices, treat their decisions with respect and openness to understanding their situation.

Reflect on a recent situation demonstrating understanding and respectful communication.

Example: Mary's friend shares increased drinking due to medical stress. Mary responds: "That operation sounds overwhelming. I care about you. Tell me more about what's happening."

## Step Three: Being Consistent

Consistency and stability over time build mutual trust. Unpredictable behavior and decisions undermine trustworthiness and decrease interpersonal effectiveness.

Identify recent situations where consistency mattered or would have benefited interactions.

Example: Despite tiredness, a husband paused a movie when his stressed wife needed to discuss work concerns. He consistently prioritizes giving her undivided attention during important conversations.
