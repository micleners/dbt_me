# Skills of Assertiveness, Part 1

Assertive communication is a core DBT skill within interpersonal effectiveness. The approach involves expressing your opinions, emotions and needs clearly, honestly and respectfully while considering others' needs.

## Knowing Your Priorities

Before requesting anything from a relationship, clarify what you actually want. The guidance suggests tuning into your [Wise Mind](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/wise-mind/) by [observing your feelings](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/observing/) to identify what's missing or desired.

Consider these questions:

- Do you need support or want to provide it?
- Would you like more time together?
- Do boundaries need clarification?

Document your relationship priorities and rate their importance on a 1-5 scale.

## Asking For What You Want

Describe the situation objectively without judgment or blame. Express your feelings and assertively state your needs, remembering that maintaining the relationship matters alongside requesting what you want.

The guidance recommends avoiding judgmental statements like "You are..." and instead using phrases such as "I want..." or "I need..." to convey respect and openness.

## Negotiation

Recognize both parties' needs as valid (unless they violate basic interpersonal rights). This step emphasizes creating "an atmosphere of mutual trust and respect" by demonstrating willingness to cooperate and find compromise.
