# FAST Skill

Standing up for yourself can feel impossible: be too assertive and you risk hurting others, but back down and you lose self-respect. The FAST skill helps navigate this tension. It's a DBT interpersonal effectiveness approach for expressing your needs while maintaining personal integrity, avoiding excessive apologies or over-explaining.

## FAST Meaning

The acronym breaks down as follows:

- F: Fair
- A: No Apologies
- S: Stick to Your Values
- T: Truthful

## How to Do FAST Skill

**Fair**: Treat yourself and others with respect; stay honest and calm without exaggeration or manipulation. Monitor your tone, body language, and word choice. Remain respectful while avoiding blame or assumptions.

Example: "I know you're upset about the missed deadline, and I want to explain what happened clearly."

**No Apologies**: Don't apologize for having needs, setting boundaries, sharing opinions, or existing. Eliminate self-deprecating phrases like "I'm sorry to bother you" or "I hate to ask." Replace them with direct statements: "I need to set a boundary here" or "I'll need to reschedule."

**Stick to Your Values**: Prioritize long-term integrity over short-term comfort. When pressured, reconnect with what matters most. Ask yourself whether you're acting from fear or integrity rather than smoothing things over temporarily.

**Truthful**: Be direct and honest without cruelty; avoid manipulation. Communicate clearly without exaggeration or dishonesty.

Example: "I don't have bandwidth for this right now" demonstrates honesty without cruelty.

## Applying FAST to Your Life

**Scenario**: A coworker repeatedly assigns their work to you.

- Fair: "I know things are busy for both of us."
- No Apologies: "I can't take on any extra tasks right now."
- Stick to Your Values: "I value fairness and clear boundaries at work."
- Truthful: "If I keep covering for you, my own deadlines suffer."

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does this mean I should never apologize?**

Not at all. Apologize when you've genuinely hurt someone or made a mistake. FAST discourages apologizing for existing or having different opinions.

**What if I feel guilty sticking to my values?**

This reaction is common for people-pleasers. Start small and acknowledge each time you speak honestly and respectfully.

**Is FAST useful for conflicts with loved ones?**

Yes, particularly if you tend to back down or over-explain. It maintains kindness while grounding your position in integrity.

**What if the other person becomes upset anyway?**

You cannot control their reactions: only communicate with clarity and integrity. FAST helps you stay composed regardless of their response.

**How do I distinguish honesty from reactive responses?**

Use [STOP](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/emotion-regulation/stop-skill/) to pause before responding, then apply [Wise Mind](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/wise-mind/) to communicate clearly without impulsivity.

**What if I'm unsure how to express myself honestly?**

Try the [GIVE Skill](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/interpersonal-effectiveness/give-skill/) which teaches gentle, respectful communication without sugarcoating truth.

**What if I don't know my own values?**

The [Life Worth Living](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/supplementary/life-worth-living/) lesson helps clarify what matters to you for more confident communication.

**Can I use FAST when I've made a mistake?**

Yes. FAST teaches self-respect, including being honest about mistakes without excessive self-blame. [Radical Acceptance](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/distress-tolerance/radical-acceptance/) can address guilt or shame.
