# Skills of Assertiveness Part 2

This guide completes the six-step assertiveness framework for interpersonal effectiveness in DBT. Building on [Skills of Assertiveness, Part 1](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/interpersonal-effectiveness/skills-of-assertiveness/), this section addresses the final three skills: obtaining information, declining requests without harming relationships, and staying aligned with personal values.

## Getting Information

The fourth assertiveness skill focuses on understanding the other person's perspective. Rather than making assumptions, practice asking genuine questions about their thoughts, emotions, and needs. This aligns with [mindfulness principles](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/) by encouraging present-moment awareness and authentic curiosity.

Key approach: Ask open-ended questions to discover where the other person stands on your request.

Example: Ben asks his boss: "What do you think about my request? I am curious to find out what your thoughts are on this."

## Saying No Without Damaging Relationships

Setting healthy boundaries through assertive refusal protects both your wellbeing and the relationship's long-term quality. Passive or aggressive communication undermines this goal.

Example response: "I understand our competitive environment requires efficiency. However, constant overwork risks my productivity and burnout. Can we find a solution that allows quality work?"

## Acting According to Your Values

Consider two dimensions of values:

1. Honest motivation behind your request
2. Relationship quality you wish to foster

Aligning communication with your core values whether mental health, integrity, or personal boundaries strengthens authenticity.

Example: Ben identifies that mental health and quality work performance are fundamental values driving his request.
