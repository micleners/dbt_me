# Barriers to Interpersonal Effectiveness

This DBT exercise addresses common obstacles that hinder effective communication, stemming from learned communication styles. There is no such thing as a perfect way of communicating, and skill development is an ongoing journey.

## Barrier I: Old Patterns

Communication habits developed through upbringing and past relationships often lean toward aggressive or passive styles rather than assertiveness.

Aggressive patterns include:

- Discounting others' needs
- Blaming others for conflicts
- Belittling others' viewpoints
- Derailing conversations away from others' emotions
- Withdrawing or making threats
- Guilt-tripping
- Punishing or threatening punishment

Passive patterns often involve becoming silent during conflicts due to fear of worsening the situation. While providing short-term relief, passive responses fail to communicate actual needs or create lasting satisfaction.

## Barrier II: Identifying Needs

Conflict can obscure awareness of actual objectives. The solution involves taking time to prioritize and clearly articulate what you need from the interaction.

## Barrier III: Negative Predictions

Fear-based thinking creates catastrophic scenarios. Recommended approaches include:

- Evaluating evidence for and against feared outcomes
- Developing coping strategies if fears materialize

## Barrier IV: Overwhelming Emotions

Individuals from conflict-heavy backgrounds may experience automatic emotional overwhelm. The exercise recommends:

- Observing physical signs (racing heart, tension, flushing)
- Practicing mindful diaphragmatic breathing

## Barrier V: Toxic Relationships

When others consistently use aggressive communication, strategy involves:

- Centering yourself in your wise mind
- Anticipating behavior patterns
- Preparing assertive responses in advance
