# Resistance and Conflict

This DBT Interpersonal Effectiveness exercise addresses communication challenges when encountering resistance from others. The module teaches strategies to maintain effective dialogue despite disagreement or opposition.

## Skill One: Validating the Other Person's View

Validation demonstrates understanding without requiring agreement. Using the framework "I understand that [their perspective]. On my part [your perspective]," you acknowledge their feelings while expressing your own needs.

Example: A manager might say: "I understand you're overwhelmed with other responsibilities. On my part, we need to complete this project on time to meet our commitment."

## Skill Two: Repeating Your Point of View

When someone dominates conversation with counterarguments, develop a brief, clear statement of your position and repeat it calmly with minimal variation. Avoid asking additional questions that might reopen debate.

Example: Consistently restating "We should present a unified approach to our daughter's behavior" despite receiving pushback.

## Skill Three: Asking for Specifics

Request clarification when criticism feels vague: "What exactly is bothering you about...?" This technique uncovers underlying concerns and provides valuable information for meaningful dialogue.

Example: Asking "What exactly bothers you about my guys' night out?" may reveal the actual concern: fear of emotional distance in the relationship.

## Key Insight

Not validating what the other person is feeling, thinking or needing can lead to one-sided communication or relationships.
