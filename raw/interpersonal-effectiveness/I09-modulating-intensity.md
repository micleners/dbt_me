# Modulating Intensity

Modulating intensity is a DBT interpersonal skill referring to how strongly we request something from someone else. It helps individuals calibrate their assertiveness when making requests, neither passive nor aggressive, by considering two key factors.

## Step One: Level of Urgency of Your Need

Assess how urgent your request is on a scale of 0-100%. Consider whether the need must be addressed immediately or can be postponed, and evaluate your flexibility around timing and outcomes.

## Step Two: Level of Vulnerability

Evaluate the other person's or relationship's vulnerability (0-100%). Reflect on whether they're experiencing stress, major life challenges, or emotional difficulty that might affect how they receive your request.

## Step Three: Adjusting the Level of Intensity

Based on your assessments, determine whether to modify your request's intensity. A high-urgency need paired with low vulnerability might warrant a direct approach, while lower urgency with higher vulnerability suggests a gentler presentation.

## Practical Example

A parent requests their son clean his bedroom. Despite high urgency (60%), the son's vulnerability due to friendship troubles (60%) suggested a milder manner that acknowledged his emotional state while still making the request.

This technique works best for important requests where uncertainty exists. With practice, evaluating these factors becomes intuitive rather than requiring explicit calculation.
