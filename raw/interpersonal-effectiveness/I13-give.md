# GIVE Skill

The GIVE skill is a DBT interpersonal effectiveness tool designed for communicating important messages while protecting relationships. Unlike [DEAR MAN](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/interpersonal-effectiveness/dear-man/), which emphasizes assertiveness, GIVE prioritizes kindness and connection. It works best when asking for something, declining requests, or expressing yourself without alienating others: applicable to friendships, romantic partnerships, family discussions, and professional contexts requiring warmth.

## What GIVE Means

- G: Be Gentle
- I: Act Interested
- V: Validate
- E: Use an Easy manner

This acronym addresses maintaining relationships while standing firm on your needs, particularly when connection matters more than "winning."

## How to Do GIVE Skill

### G - Be Gentle

Avoid attacks, threats, and blame. Maintain a calm tone and relaxed body language. Gentleness differs from passivity: it means avoiding unnecessary escalation. Skip sarcasm, eye-rolling, or shutdown tactics. Don't "dig for guilt" or issue ultimatums.

Example: Replace "You never help out around here" with "It would really help if you could take care of the dishes tonight." Breathe before responding if tension rises; your composure sets the conversation's tone.

### I - Act Interested

Display genuine care for the other person's perspective, even during disagreement. Listen without interrupting and create space for their viewpoint. Use open body language, nod occasionally, and ask questions while staying mentally present rather than rehearsing rebuttals.

Example: "I hear you're feeling stressed about work, that makes sense." This demonstrates basic respect without requiring agreement.

### V - Validate

Acknowledge the other person's feelings even if disapproving of their actions. Validation helps people feel understood, reducing defensiveness.

Examples include: "I get why you'd feel that way," "That must have been frustrating," or "I can see how that upset you." This single step frequently softens conversations dramatically.

### E - Use an Easy Manner

Remain approachable and warm when possible. Incorporate humor appropriately. Relax your facial expression, voice, and posture. Even during difficult conversations, elements like soft tones or slight smiles maintain humanity. Use casual language when it helps the message land effectively.

Example: Try "Hey, can we chat for a second about something that's been on my mind?" instead of "We need to have a serious talk." This keeps the other person receptive.

## Applying GIVE to Your Life

**Situation:** You want your friend to stop canceling plans at the last minute.

Using GIVE:

- G: "I'm not upset, and this isn't an attack, just want to share how I've been feeling."
- I: "I know your schedule has been wild lately, and I want to understand."
- V: "It makes sense that you've been overwhelmed, it's a lot to juggle."
- E: "Can we figure out a way to make our plans work better for both of us?"

This method makes hearing your perspective easier for the other person without triggering defensiveness.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Isn't this just sugarcoating?**

GIVE isn't about hiding truth: it's about delivering it respectfully. Directness and assertiveness remain possible; only harshness and reactivity disappear.

**What if the other person is being rude or aggressive?**

Use GIVE to maintain composure, though tolerating disrespect isn't required. Combine GIVE with [FAST](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/interpersonal-effectiveness/fast-skill/) or [DEAR MAN](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/interpersonal-effectiveness/dear-man/) to hold boundaries while staying calm.

**What if I feel fake being "easy" or "gentle"?**

Initial discomfort is typical when building skills. A calm tone and validating others will eventually feel natural, improving relationship outcomes.

**Can I use GIVE in text messages or emails?**

Absolutely. Write kindly and clearly, avoiding accusations, ALL CAPS, or sarcasm. Maintain a human tone throughout.

**What if I lose my cool despite trying GIVE?**

Use [STOP](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/emotion-regulation/stop-skill/) first to ground yourself before conversations start. GIVE functions better when your nervous system isn't activated.

**How do I validate someone when I totally disagree with them?**

Validation doesn't require agreement. Apply [Wise Mind](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/wise-mind/) to see both perspectives and respond without fueling conflict.

**What if the relationship feels unsafe or one-sided?**

GIVE functions optimally in mutually respectful relationships. When mutual respect is absent, consider [FAST](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/interpersonal-effectiveness/fast-skill/) or practice [Radical Acceptance](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/distress-tolerance/radical-acceptance/) if change isn't achievable.

**How do I stay present during hard conversations?**

Practice [Observe](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/observing/) and [Describe](https://dialecticalbehaviortherapy.com/mindfulness/describe-your-emotions/) from the Mindfulness module. Focus on the person, their words, and your body sensations without immediate judgment or reaction.
