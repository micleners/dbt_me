# Interpersonal Boundaries

This DBT interpersonal effectiveness exercise helps individuals understand and manage the boundaries that exist in their relationships. As social beings, people need connection while maintaining individual identities through thoughtful boundary management.

## Three Types of Interpersonal Boundaries

**Psychological Boundaries:** Control over sharing personal information, opinions, thoughts, and beliefs with others.

**Emotional Boundaries:** Determining how much others influence your emotional state and whether you allow emotional manipulation.

**Physical Boundaries:** Decisions about touch, personal space, and sexual activity with specific individuals.

## Being Mindful of the Situation

The exercise recommends observing how much and what type of information others share, then responding accordingly. You should choose to open up your boundaries to people who also trust you with theirs. This reciprocal approach helps determine appropriate flexibility levels.

## Why Practice Boundaries

Two primary reasons support boundary work:

1. Protect yourself and self-esteem through stronger boundaries
2. Allow relationships to grow via flexible boundaries when appropriate

## Negotiating Boundaries

Boundary negotiations occur both implicitly and explicitly. Examples include:

- Implicitly: Redirecting conversations or remaining quiet about boundary concerns
- Explicitly: Directly stating boundary preferences to the other person

Boundaries extend beyond the three main types to include topics like religion and money. Temporary discomfort during boundary adjustment is normal and part of progress.
