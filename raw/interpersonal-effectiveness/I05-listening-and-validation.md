# Listening and Validation

This DBT interpersonal effectiveness exercise focuses on developing attentive listening skills and validation techniques. Effective communication requires acknowledging both ourselves and others, moving beyond assertiveness to create genuine connection.

## Step One: Asking Questions and Reflective Listening

The foundation involves demonstrating genuine curiosity about another person's experience. Practitioners should ask clarifying questions and reflect back what they hear using phrases like "I understand that..." or "I can hear that you feel..."

Key practices:

- Show interest in their perspective
- Use reflective statements to confirm understanding
- Ask follow-up questions to demonstrate engagement

## Step Two: Body Language and Identifying with Others

Pay attention to nonverbal cues that may reveal what someone truly feels. Look for body posture, eye contact patterns, and overall openness. When possible, identify shared experiences that help the other person feel understood and connected.

## Step Three: Acceptance and Encouraging Participation

Validation means accepting others' thoughts and feelings even when you don't fully understand them. Maintain nonjudgmental attitudes, keep communicating through disconnects, and recognize that different people have different experiences.
