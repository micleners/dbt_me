# Assertiveness Scripts and Interpersonal Rights

This DBT interpersonal effectiveness exercise provides templates for communicating thoughts, feelings, and needs assertively. It combines assertiveness scripts with exploration of fundamental interpersonal rights.

## Assertiveness Scripts

The exercise offers three communication templates:

**"I think..." Statement**

Express factual observations and perceptions without judgment or assumptions about others' thoughts or emotions.

Example: "I think we've been ordering takeout frequently lately and could benefit from cooking at home more often."

**"I feel..." Statement**

Communicate your emotional experience without attacking the other person, which often triggers defensive responses.

Example: "I feel anxious about our future and would appreciate discussing long-term plans."

**"I want..." Statement**

Present specific, behavioral requests rather than asking someone to change their feelings or thoughts. Keep requests singular and achievable.

Example: "I want to spend tonight together so we can reconnect."

## Interpersonal Rights

Everyone has the right to:

- Express feelings authentically
- Disagree respectfully
- Decline requests without guilt
- Set boundaries
- Take time for decision-making
- Receive respectful treatment

## Key Considerations

Using "you make me feel" phrasing typically triggers defensiveness. When conversations stall despite genuine effort, prioritize self-care and identify concrete next steps for resolution.

Assertiveness requires patient, consistent practice and doesn't guarantee outcomes, but gradually improves relationship quality.
