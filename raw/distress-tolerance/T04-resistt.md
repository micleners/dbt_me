# RESISTT Technique: DBT Skills for Managing Overwhelming Emotions

## Introduction

RESISTT is a collection of seven techniques designed to help you cope with overwhelming emotions. According to the resource, "When you have an experience of overwhelming emotions it's hard not to act on unproductive urges ranging from minor (e.g. overeating) to serious (e.g. self-harm)." This technique, covered in Sheri Van Dijk's book *DBT Made Simple*, helps you manage unhealthy urges without acting on them.

## When to Use

Use RESISTT when faced with an unhealthy urge or overwhelming emotions. Select the techniques most appropriate and helpful for your specific situation.

## Seven Steps

### Step One: Reframing the Situation

When overwhelmed, people often think in black-and-white terms. Reframing means changing your perspective to find "the shades of grey between the white and the black." For every difficult situation, you can objectively find realistic arguments that show it's not as definitive as it initially seems. This helps you think more realistically without invalidating your genuine hardships.

### Step Two: Engaging in a Distracting Activity

Use "list of distracting, pleasurable activities" to mindfully engage in something that shifts your focus from the distressing situation. The principle is that "Saying to yourself 'Stop thinking about so-and-so' will just make you think more about that thing."

### Step Three: Someone Else

Refocus your attention on another person. Help a friend, surprise a loved one, volunteer, or listen non-judgmentally to someone's story. This shift redirects your attention away from overwhelming emotions.

### Step Four: Intense Sensations

Distract your mind with safe, intense sensations when experiencing painful emotions. Examples include:
- Taking a cold or hot shower or bath
- Holding an ice cube
- Walking outside in extreme weather
- Sitting in intense sunlight

The founder of DBT, Marsha Linehan, suggests "holding an ice-cube can replace self-harming behaviors or satisfy those urges."

### Step Five: Shut it Out

Leave environments contributing to your distress when possible. If you continue dwelling on the negative situation, "ask yourself whether this is a problem that you could solve right now. If it is - do that." If not solvable immediately, visualize putting your problem in a box to address later.

### Step Six: Neutral Thoughts

Engage in mentally neutral activities that won't amplify distressing emotions:
- Count to ten or count your breaths
- Observe environmental details like colors
- Remember and sing your favorite song
- Repeat helpful phrases like "Right now, it is what it is, I accept everything"

### Step Seven: Take a Break

Postpone work or chores inconsequentially, take a day off for self-care, or simply rest. Use this technique cautiously to avoid creating new problems through excessive postponement.

## Example Scenario

**Situation:** An evening with your girlfriend becomes uncomfortable when she makes jokes about your outfit. You feel ridiculed and judged, with an urge to chain-smoke.

**Application:**

1. **Reframing:** Recognize you may have jumped to conclusions; her jokes might not be intentionally hurtful, and your sensitivity about appearance stems from personal insecurity.

2. **Distracting activity:** Play video games to relax.

3. **Someone else:** Call a friend to chat.

4. **Intense sensations:** Take a cold walk outside in November weather; possibly invite your friend.

5. **Shut it out:** Explain to your girlfriend you need personal time, then take that walk rather than escalate into an argument.

6. **Neutral thoughts:** Counter black-and-white thinking with "It's okay, I accept this situation."

7. **Take a break:** Use previous steps to manage the urge without needing additional breaks from routine.

## FAQs

**How can I better reframe the situation?**

In overwhelming situations, people think in black-and-white terms, but objective arguments always exist against such thinking. When struggling with self-compassion, "imagine you are talking to a friend. What would you say to them, to a person that you really care about?"

**Why trying not to think about something doesn't work?**

"If you actively try not to think about something, you are probably constantly reminding yourself of that thing." Resisting unwanted thoughts often backfires. Instead, distract yourself with pleasurable activities. For quieter depression or anxiety, try accepting thoughts first, then finding solutions.

**Should I always use this technique when trying to manage urges?**

The Distress Tolerance module offers several exercises. After becoming familiar with all techniques, choose what suits you best in each situation.
