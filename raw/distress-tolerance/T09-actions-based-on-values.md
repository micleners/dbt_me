# Actions Based on Values: DBT Exercise T9

## Introduction

The concept of aligning actions with personal values can enhance DBT treatment outcomes and overall life satisfaction. According to the resource, "Having a strong sense of purpose can make emotional distress more manageable." By identifying core values and developing strategies to live according to them, individuals can build resilience during difficult times. The exercise helps people recognize what matters most and create concrete steps toward value-aligned living.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Step One: Identify Your Top Three Life Values

Review this list of common life aspects and select the three most important to you:

1. Romantic relationships (dating, marriage)
2. Family (relationships beyond marriage/parenting)
3. Work (career advancement, finances)
4. Socializing and friends
5. Educational goals
6. Spirituality or religion
7. Health and self-care
8. Community life (charity, service)
9. Parenting
10. Recreation and fun

### Step Two: Clarify Why These Values Matter

For each selected value, write down the specific reasons it's meaningful to you. The resource provides this example from "Emily": examining work value involves recognizing "how productive I feel after finishing a project" and valuing "financial security and independence."

### Step Three: Define Concrete Actions

Develop specific, realistic actions aligned with your values. Rather than vague goals, identify exact activities and timing. Emily's example included: "next week on Monday, I'll start searching on the Internet something that would be interesting and challenging for me to do from home."

## FAQ Section

**Q: I have more than three important values—how should I choose?**

Rate values on a scale of 1-10 and select the top three based on which provide the greatest fulfillment. You can repeat this exercise in a few months as priorities may shift.

**Q: What if I lack time for these activities?**

Start with smaller, realistic activities requiring minimal time commitment. For example, instead of frequent outings, commit to socializing "once a week" intentionally.

**Q: I'm depressed and don't feel motivated.**

"Starting small and setting realistic goals" builds momentum. Begin with activities requiring less energy, commit despite low motivation, and recognize that "breaking the cycle of avoidance and withdrawal can be key to building momentum."
