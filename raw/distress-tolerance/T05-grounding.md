# Grounding Technique (T5): DBT Distress Tolerance Exercise

## Introduction

The grounding technique aims to "help you get in the here and now, and shift your focus away from distressing thoughts, emotions, or sensations." When overwhelmed, people often drift into rumination about the past or future rather than remaining present. Grounding reconnects you with the current moment and your immediate environment.

The critical first step is recognizing when your thoughts have wandered and you've lost presence. Once you identify this avoidance pattern, you can intentionally apply grounding techniques to return to reality.

## When to Use

Apply grounding techniques during moments of emotional overwhelm or when you notice yourself becoming disconnected from the present moment.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step One: Describe What You See (10 minutes)**

Observe your visual surroundings carefully. Describe objects' colors, shapes, and characteristics. Note whether the space is light or dark, spacious or compact. Linger with each object before moving to the next.

*Example provided:* "I can see my white wallet lying on the desk. It has a rectangular shape and there is a sunray touching it..." and continues with detailed observations of other items.

**Step Two: Breathing (5 minutes)**

Focus on your breath. Notice air filling your body as you inhale and relaxation as you exhale. Count breaths from one to five on the exhale. Begin with deep, slow breathing, then settle into your natural rhythm.

**Step Three: Mindfulness of the Body (5 minutes)**

Observe physical contact points: weight of legs on the chair, back against the chair, feet on the floor. Notice any tension, pain, or comfort throughout your body. Feel the weight of your arms resting in your lap or on the chair.

**Step Four: Engaging Other Senses (5 minutes)**

Notice smells in your environment—whether distinct or absent. Listen to nearby and distant sounds. Remain open to whatever you perceive through smell and sound.

## FAQ

**How does this differ from mindfulness exercises?**

Grounding techniques address immediate emotional crises by shifting attention away from distressing thoughts. Mindfulness is a daily practice that gradually builds present-moment awareness and non-judgmental observation. Mindfulness develops curiosity and acceptance, while grounding creates stability during acute distress.

**I can't recognize when I'm avoiding reality, so I remember the skill too late.**

Practice the mindfulness exercises first. They build awareness of your internal and external experiences, making it easier to notice when you're disconnecting and need grounding.

**Won't grounding be difficult during an emotional crisis?**

Initially, resistance may occur. However, this skill improves daily functioning and can alleviate emotional distress. Stay open to its benefits.
