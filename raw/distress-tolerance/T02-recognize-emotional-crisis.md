# Recognizing Signs of an Emotional Crisis: Exercises & Worksheets

## Introduction

To prevent and manage emotional crises effectively, you must first develop awareness of what occurs before and during distress. Building on your mindfulness practice, you can now observe and describe warning indicators, emotions, thoughts, and behaviors surrounding crisis situations. Most people experience similar patterns across different circumstances. Understanding these patterns—along with other skills in this module—enables you to address crisis situations without escalating them.

## Instructions

This exercise teaches you to identify when you're entering an emotional crisis so you can interrupt the pattern. Practice this exercise three times over the next week.

### Step One: Recall Your Last Emotional Crisis

Think of your most recent emotional crisis—a time of intense, overwhelming negative emotions where you felt unable to cope. The situation can be anything that made you feel helpless in that moment. You don't need perfect details yet; this exercise helps you develop that awareness gradually.

### Step Two: Emotions, Thoughts, and Behaviors

**Emotions**: Describe what you felt during the crisis. Consider whether this emotional response typically occurs in similar situations for you. Everyone's emotional experience varies—there are no "correct" feelings.

*Example*: During a conflict with a friend, feeling overwhelmed and unable to cope, experiencing a combination of rage, shame, and hurt.

**Thoughts**: Identify what you typically think before engaging in problematic behavior. Write the exact words you were thinking. Reflect on whether this thought pattern recurs in similar situations.

*Example*: "I can't believe she would criticize me like this. Everyone must be pretending. Our friendship is ruined, so why care?"

**Behaviors**: Recall what you typically do before and during the crisis. Identify specific actions. Determine whether this represents a consistent pattern or situation-specific response.

*Example*: Speaking hurtful words due to feeling hurt, becoming offensive and seemingly like a different person, later regretting actions that don't reflect your true self.

### Step Three: Sensations, Environment, and Key Triggers

**Sensations**: Describe physical sensations you experience before or during the crisis. Intense emotions typically produce noticeable bodily manifestations.

*Example*: Racing pulse, warm and flushed face, mild headache from rising anger, excess energy with no outlet.

**Environment and Key Triggers**: Describe what typically happens in your surroundings before and during crisis. Identify specific triggers causing intense emotions. Recognizing these triggers provides valuable tools for handling similar future situations.

*Example*: Feeling hurt and fearing friendship abandonment serves as the key trigger. Environmental factor is criticism—which may seem less intense than perceived, but causes an internal "snap."

## FAQs

**What does an emotional crisis consist of?**

An emotional crisis involves intense negative emotions you're unsure how to manage in the moment. During crisis, you might engage in unhelpful behaviors—drinking, overspending, substance use, or conflict—to cope. Distress tolerance skills teach you to manage crisis situations without making them worse.

**Is this exercise alone sufficient to prevent future emotional crises?**

This exercise builds mindfulness of warning signs, triggers, and typical feelings and thoughts before and during crises. However, other distress tolerance skills are equally necessary for managing emotional crises. Different skills work better in different situations. Learning multiple skills provides better resources for navigating overwhelming emotions successfully.

**What if I can't remember all aspects of the emotional crisis I chose?**

If you struggle to recall details, practice this exercise after your next emotional crisis occurs. Important note: aspects typically remain consistent across similar situations, so future awareness will help you take preventive action before emotions escalate.
