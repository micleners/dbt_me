# ACCEPTS: DBT Distress Tolerance Skill

## Introduction

"When you're in emotional pain, you don't always need a solution. You just need a way to get through the moment without making it worse." ACCEPTS is part of your Distress Tolerance toolkit, designed for times when emotions run high and nothing seems helpful. While you may not immediately fix the situation, you can manage your emotional response to prevent regrettable actions.

ACCEPTS is an acronym providing various distraction techniques when overwhelmed. Each letter represents a category of actions helping you manage emotional waves—not ignoring feelings, but buying time until you can respond more effectively.

## What ACCEPTS Stands For

- **A** – Activities
- **C** – Contributing
- **C** – Comparisons
- **E** – Emotions (opposite)
- **P** – Pushing away
- **T** – Thoughts
- **S** – Sensations

You don't need to use every strategy—even one can help you through an emotional rough patch.

## When to Use ACCEPTS

Use this skill when feeling overwhelmed, emotionally dysregulated, or approaching crisis. The goal is surviving the moment safely and skillfully, not solving the root problem.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### A: Activities

Redirect attention away from distress through action:

- Clean something small (toy shelf, drawer, sink)
- Do a puzzle or play a game
- Take a walk or bike ride
- Paint, bake, crochet, tinker, or garden

**Example:** Feeling anxious awaiting medical results, you reorganize your books for 20 minutes, providing alternative focus while reducing fear.

### C: Contributing

Help others to create purpose and shift focus outward:

- Text a friend to check in
- Volunteer your time
- Offer to help with tasks (groceries, babysitting, moving furniture)
- Write thank-you messages

### C: Comparisons

Shift perspective without invalidating pain:

- Compare current distress to tougher previous times
- Read stories about others overcoming challenges
- Remind yourself how far you've progressed

### E: Emotions (Opposite)

Generate very different emotions from current distress:

- Watch funny videos
- Listen to upbeat music
- Read inspiring stories
- Watch heartwarming or silly movies

### P: Pushing Away

Temporarily set aside distressing thoughts or problems:

- Visualize placing the problem in a container (box, drawer, envelope)
- Decide when you'll revisit it ("I'll think about this tonight")
- Shift attention to something else

### T: Thoughts (Other)

Fill your mind with structured, neutral, or manageable thoughts:

- Count backwards from 100 by sevens
- Name U.S. states, countries, animals, movies, or vegetables
- Try brain teasers or crossword puzzles
- Recite memorized lyrics, quotes, or verses

### S: Sensations (Intense)

Use safe, intense physical sensations to ground yourself:

- Hold an ice cube
- Splash cold water on your face
- Smell something strong (peppermint, citrus)
- Jump up and down or clap loudly

## How to Pick One in Distress

When experiencing intense emotion, choose just one ACCEPTS skill. Ask yourself:

- What can I do right now that feels even a little bit possible?
- What category might fit this moment best?

## FAQ

**Isn't this just avoiding the problem?**

Pausing is not pretending. ACCEPTS helps you stay grounded so you can revisit issues later with more clarity and control. Skillfully managing emotions keeps you effective.

**How long do I use these techniques?**

Until emotional intensity decreases several notches, then move to skills like Radical Acceptance or Wise Mind. These are short-term strategies, not long-term fixes.

**What if nothing seems to help?**

Try a different ACCEPTS skill or use one even if unmotivated. The point isn't feeling amazing but avoiding making situations worse.

**Can I use ACCEPTS when not in crisis?**

Absolutely. These skills help with boredom, worry, impatience, or sadness. Regular practice makes them easier to remember when truly needed.
