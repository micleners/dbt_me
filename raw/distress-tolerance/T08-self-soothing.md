# Self Soothing: DBT Distress Tolerance Skill

## Introduction

"Self-soothing is a technique that is used to manage and regulate one's emotions and create a sense of calm and comfort." This approach engages the five senses—sight, hearing, taste, touch, and smell—to produce grounding experiences and relaxation.

When facing emotional distress, activating sensory experiences can help you reach a more peaceful state where you're able to think and behave more effectively. Since everyone responds differently to various activities, experimenting to find what resonates personally is important.

## Instructions

Review the suggestions organized by sense below. Select 2-3 ideas from each group and document them in your worksheet.

### 1. Sense of Sight (Visual)

- Visit nature settings like parks to observe the interplay of colors and life
- Find and collect soothing images from the internet (cities, nature, art)
- Attend museums or galleries
- Watch cinematically beautiful films with stories you can comfortably engage with
- Create a personal collection of pleasurable images to review when needed

### 2. Sense of Hearing (Auditory)

- Speak with someone whose voice brings you happiness
- Listen to relaxing favorite music
- Explore soothing jazz, classical, opera, or new age instrumental music
- Spend time in nature enjoying ambient sounds (birds, wind, people)
- Play a musical instrument or sing
- Listen to audiobooks, podcasts, or shows you enjoy

### 3. Sense of Smell (Olfactory)

- Wear pleasant perfume or cologne
- Light scented candles
- Cook aromatic meals
- Purchase flowers or indoor plants
- Embrace someone you love whose scent calms you
- Visit locations with enjoyable scents (flower shops, bakeries, restaurants)

### 4. Sense of Taste (Gustatory)

- Prepare and slowly savor your favorite meal
- Visit favorite restaurants for preferred meals
- Enjoy comfort foods like chocolate or ice cream (in moderation)
- Prepare enjoyable beverages (coffee, tea, cocoa—avoid alcohol)
- Eat fresh fruit mindfully
- Chew gum or enjoy sweets

### 5. Sense of Touch (Tactile)

- Wrap yourself in soft blankets or feel the texture
- Pet and hold your animal companion
- Wear comfortable clothing
- Take warm or cold showers or bubble baths
- Obtain a massage or self-massage
- Touch smooth, velvety, or fluffy materials

## FAQs

**What if the situation causing distress doesn't allow these activities?**

If possible, remove yourself and calm down. If you cannot leave, practice accepting what is happening without judgment and wait for the situation to end. Afterward, try one of these activities. Alternatively, engage in small actions that won't negatively impact your circumstances (taking a work break for coffee or listening to music).

**Can I do activities not listed above?**

Yes. The worksheet includes space for your personal ideas. Since everyone differs, your unique calming activities are valid as long as they're safe for you.

**Will these activities effectively help me calm down?**

Although these activities seem simple, they're very effective. "When you are emotionally distressed it might seem like these activities are perhaps too mellow to work." Consider using distraction techniques first (like the [RESISTT](/distress-tolerance/resistt/) technique), then transition to these. Positive emotions are subtler than intense negative ones, so practicing these sensations builds the skill progressively.
