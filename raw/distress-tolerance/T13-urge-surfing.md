# Urge Surfing: DBT Distress Tolerance Skill

## Introduction

When powerful urges arise—whether to lash out, binge eat, self-harm, drink, or escape—your body and mind can feel overwhelmed. The intensity feels unbearable, creating a sense that you must act immediately or "explode."

Urge Surfing is a Distress Tolerance skill teaching you to sit with urges without acting on them. Rather than suppressing or fighting urges directly, you learn to ride them out like waves. The key insight: urges always peak and fade. By remaining present and mindful during discomfort, you develop response capability through awareness rather than force.

## Understanding Urges as Waves

Urges follow a wave pattern: building intensity, reaching a peak, then diminishing. Most people act when urges peak, believing they're unbearable. However, without reinforcing the urge through action, its strength naturally decreases.

Urge Surfing differs fundamentally from distraction. You intentionally tune into physical and mental sensations without attempting to change the wave itself. This skillful observation, grounded in mindfulness, replaces reactive patterns with conscious choice.

## 5-Minute Urge Surfing Practice

Practice this exercise whenever urges arise. You can also rehearse during calm periods to build readiness.

### Step 1: Name the Urge

Identify what you're experiencing the urge to do. State it aloud or write it clearly using the format, "I have the urge to…" This practice creates awareness and space between impulse and response. Examples include "I have the urge to yell at my partner" or "I have the urge to check my ex's profile again." Honest acknowledgment represents a powerful initial step.

### Step 2: Observe the Urge Without Judgment

Direct attention to how the urge manifests physically. Notice tightness in your chest, facial heat, hand restlessness, or other sensations. Allow yourself to experience it fully without attempting to fix or escape it. Adopt a scientist's perspective, observing with curiosity rather than emotional intensity. Your task involves noticing the urge, not stopping it.

### Step 3: Breathe and Surf

Visualize the urge as a rising and falling wave. Focus on steady, slow breathing. Each breath helps you maintain balance as the wave passes. Don't resist the feeling or struggle against it; permit it to arrive and depart naturally. Picture it gently crashing ashore and slowly receding. Like all waves, this one has limited duration.

### Step 4: Remind Yourself: It Will Pass

As you breathe and ride the wave, gently repeat to yourself: "This urge is not permanent." Tell yourself, "I am not the urge. I can ride this out. This is hard, but I've done hard things before." Set a two-to-five-minute timer and commit to staying present until intensity fades. Most urges begin diminishing within this window.

## Reflect Afterwards

After the urge passes (or if you acted on it), pause and reflect by asking yourself:

- What did I notice about the urge?
- Did it pass?
- What helped me stay with it?
- What would I try differently next time?

### Real-Life Example

**Scenario:** You feel an intense urge to text your ex after seeing a memory notification.

**Old pattern:** Send the text, experience regret afterward.

**Urge Surfing approach:**

You state: "I have the urge to reconnect."

You observe: tight chest, rapid heartbeat, buzzing in your fingers.

You breathe while imagining the urge rising as a wave.

You remind yourself: "I won't always feel like this."

Five minutes pass. Intensity decreases.

You journal your feelings instead of texting.

You stayed present without acting. That's success.

## FAQs

**Isn't this just distraction in disguise?**

No. Urge Surfing involves staying intentionally with the urge rather than avoiding it. You're simply choosing not to act. This represents mindfulness application, not avoidance.

**How does this differ from white-knuckling?**

White-knuckling involves resistance and tension. Urge Surfing means accepting the urge's presence and allowing it to proceed without reactive behavior. You observe rather than battle.

**What if I still act on the urge?**

That's acceptable. This is a learnable skill, not a guaranteed outcome. Each practice, regardless of "success," builds awareness—a valuable outcome. Learn from the experience and attempt again.

**Do urges always fade?**

Yes, consistently. While urges may return, each wave rises and subsides. No urge persists indefinitely. With practice, waves become shorter and less intense.

**Can I combine this with other skills?**

Absolutely. Pair Urge Surfing with:

- Mindful breathing
- Cold temperature (TIPP ice cube technique)
- Wise Mind check-ins
- Half Smile
- Distraction techniques (ACCEPTS) after surfing the wave
