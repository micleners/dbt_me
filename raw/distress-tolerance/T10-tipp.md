# TIPP: Distress Tolerance Technique

## Introduction

"The TIPP technique is another method that you can use to cope with overwhelming emotions and reduce distress in the moment." It serves as a first-line intervention during crisis or extreme emotional overwhelm.

The technique works best when emotional mind dominates over wise mind. TIPP stands for four sequential components designed to shift emotional state and reduce acute distress. Importantly, "TIPP is not a long-term solution, but rather a temporary tool to help you navigate acute or overwhelming distress."

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### T: Temperature

Adjust body temperature to regulate heart rate:

**For anxiety/panic:** Use cold exposure—splash face with cold water, take a cool (not extreme) shower, hold ice cubes, or walk outside in chilly weather. Cold temperatures decrease elevated heart rates.

**For depression/sadness:** Use warmth—take hot baths, wrap in blankets, spend time in warm weather, or drink warm tea. Heat increases lower heart rates.

**Important caveat:** Avoid extreme temperatures if you have blood pressure concerns. Consult your physician if necessary.

### I: Intense Exercise

Expend built-up emotional energy through cardio for 10-15 minutes:

- Running around the block
- Jumping jacks indoors
- Fast-paced walking
- Jumping rope
- Dancing
- Weight lifting

"When you spend that conserved energy you will feel more tired and your overwhelming emotions will become more balanced."

### P: Paced Breathing

Control physical manifestations (racing heart, flushed face, dry mouth, sweating):

Breathe in deeply through your nose using abdominal breathing for four seconds, then exhale through your mouth for six seconds. Continue for 1-2 minutes to decrease breathing rate.

### P: Progressive Muscle Relaxation

Release tension and excessive energy from muscles:

Work systematically through your body from top to bottom:

1. Upper back: Tighten muscles deliberately for five seconds, then release
2. Arms
3. Abdominal and back muscles
4. Buttocks
5. Thighs and upper legs
6. Calves

Feel tension decrease as muscles loosen.

## Example Scenario

**Situation:** Individual wakes feeling empty and depressed, triggered by gloomy weather and memories of past depression and dysfunctional family relationship from two years prior. Emotional spiral occurs early morning, leaving person unmotivated.

**T - Temperature:** Took a hot bath as self-kindness act; prevented deeper depression spiral

**I - Intense Exercise:** Put on music and danced for 10 minutes; gained renewed energy

**P - Paced Breathing:** Completed two minutes of paced breathing technique

**P - Progressive Muscle Relaxation:** Finished with systematic muscle relaxation while seated

**Result:** Felt significantly better; emotional regulation improved enough to continue daily responsibilities

## FAQs

**Q: Must I complete all four steps?**

A: You may feel better after initial steps, which indicates effectiveness. However, completing all steps (approximately 30 minutes total) maximizes benefits.

**Q: How do I motivate myself when emotionally overwhelmed?**

A: Practice TIPP during calm periods so you recognize its value. "First, prepare and make a plan that you will try this exercise next time when you are emotionally overwhelmed since it might save you a lot of unnecessary spent energy." Compare effort invested against suffering from overwhelming emotions.

**Q: What if cardio makes me overstimulated?**

A: Avoid overdoing any component. "Keep that in mind and try to do this exercise in a way that best suits you." Cardio triggers dopamine release (mood elevation), so moderate intensity appropriately during crisis response rather than pursuing fitness gains.
