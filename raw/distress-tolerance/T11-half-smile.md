# Half Smile: DBT Distress Tolerance Skill

## Introduction

When overwhelmed by intense emotions like anger, sadness, anxiety, or shame, your body often reflects this tension—face tight, shoulders raised, jaw clenched. Half Smile is a body-based distress tolerance skill that leverages the principle that "the body also sends signals to the brain, not just the other way around." By making a subtle adjustment to your facial expression, you can communicate to your brain that you're safe enough to relax and breathe through difficulty.

This isn't about forcing false happiness or denying pain. Instead, it gently loosens your grip on painful emotions through physical adjustment, without suppression or denial.

## Instructions

### Step One: How To Do a Half Smile

A Half Smile is a "slight, subtle softening of the mouth into a gentle smile. Not a full grin. Not a performative, toothy smile." Think of the Mona Lisa. The approach involves:

- Sit or stand in a relaxed position
- Breathe naturally
- Allow the corners of your mouth to lift slightly
- Avoid forcing the expression; aim for ease
- Hold the Half Smile for a few breaths

Initial awkwardness is normal—the goal is signaling to your brain, not pretending happiness.

### Step Two: Practice in a Neutral Situation

Before applying this skill during distress, practice when calm to build familiarity:

- Find a quiet moment when experiencing no intense emotion
- Sit comfortably and take a breath
- Form a Half Smile
- Hold it for a full minute while focusing on your breath
- Notice physical sensations, emotional changes, or thoughts that arise

Practice for 1 minute, five times weekly.

### Step Three: Apply Half Smile in Challenging Emotions

Once comfortable with the basics, apply it during mild to moderate emotional distress—situations like waiting in traffic or receiving passive-aggressive communication:

- When you notice your body tensing during emotional discomfort, pause
- Take a breath and form a Half Smile
- Focus on relaxing your face and breathing through the emotion
- Avoid engaging unhelpful thoughts; simply observe and allow the experience
- For intense emotions like grief, panic, or deep anger, expect smaller shifts—sometimes the achievement is simply not escalating the moment

### Step Four: Reflect on the Experience

Integration and future use benefit from reflection:

- What physical changes did you notice?
- Did your emotional intensity shift, even slightly?
- Were you able to pause or interrupt your automatic reaction?

Effects may be subtle initially, but consistency strengthens the brain-body coordination for managing emotional stress.

## FAQs

**Is Half Smile just pretending I feel okay when I don't?**

No. This skill is "not about faking happiness. It's about creating the conditions in your body that help you feel calmer." You're nudging your nervous system toward peace without dishonesty.

**Why does this even work?**

Half Smile draws on the facial feedback hypothesis—your facial expressions influence emotions, not just reflect them. This expression may stimulate brain regions involved in mood regulation, including the parasympathetic nervous system, supporting emotional balance over time.

**What if I don't feel anything change when I do it?**

That's acceptable. Changes are often subtle rather than dramatic. Like many DBT skills, consistency matters more than immediate results.

**How does this skill connect to the rest of DBT?**

Half Smile is part of the distress tolerance module. When using it, you practice non-judgmental awareness and radical acceptance—acknowledging emotion without escalation or avoidance.

**Can I use Half Smile with other DBT skills?**

Absolutely. It pairs well with mindful breathing, observing thoughts without judgment, Wise Mind, and Radical Acceptance, functioning as a gentle foundation beneath more active coping strategies.
