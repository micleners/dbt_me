# Distracting Activities: DBT Skills Exercise

## Introduction

During an emotional crisis, implementing coping strategies can prevent escalation. This exercise focuses on creating a list of distracting, pleasurable activities that help you "tolerate emotional distress." These activities provide space between yourself and the problematic situation, allowing you to return to challenges when you feel more stable.

## Instructions

### Step One: Things You Enjoy Doing

Identify activities you naturally enjoy during free time—hobbies that help you unwind. Suggestions include reading, watching movies, playing with pets, or listening to music.

**Key considerations:**
- Choose activities feasible on short notice
- Avoid activities you'd regret later (excessive spending, binge eating, intoxicated communication)
- Write down 5 initial activities

### Step Two: Suggested Distracting Activities

Review this comprehensive list and check activities that appeal to you:

Cook your favorite meal, coffee with friends, work out, listen to music/dance, watch movies or TV, yoga/Pilates, bubble bath, visit museums, play instruments, take walks, play games with friends, call loved ones, chat online, shopping, write a book, meditate, read magazines, get massages, watch sports, garden, visit movies/plays, read books, play video games, play with pets, date your loved one, picnic/hiking, nap/sleep, listen to podcasts, start a diary, karaoke, eat snacks, enjoy sunshine, watch YouTube, read comics, drive or use public transit, create art, have lunch out, draw/color, do chores, buy gifts, swim, dress nicely, watch comedy.

### Step Three: Create Your Personal List

Select 15-20 activities from your preferences and the suggested list. **Be specific:** instead of "listen to music," identify particular songs or create playlists in advance. Greater specificity makes implementation easier during distress.

## FAQ

**When should I use this list?**

"You can use the list whenever you feel like you can benefit from distracting yourself from overwhelming emotions." Apply these activities when tempted toward problematic behaviors, then revisit the original situation when calmer. This list will also support the RESISTT technique exercise.

**What if I enjoy unhealthy activities?**

Fill your list with activities lacking negative consequences. Activities like addictions "bring temporary relief to the pain that you might feel, but they will not solve the root of the problem." This practice develops capacity for delaying immediate gratification.

**I'm depressed and can't identify enjoyable activities.**

Before trying an activity, rate your assumed enjoyment (0-100). After attempting it, rate actual enjoyment. The second rating may exceed your assumption. Continue testing activities until finding those you rate highly.
