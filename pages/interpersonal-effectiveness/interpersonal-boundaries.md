People need connection and their own identity at once; boundaries manage the balance.

**Three types:**

- **Psychological:** what personal information, opinions, thoughts, and beliefs you share.
- **Emotional:** how much others affect your emotional state, and whether you allow emotional manipulation.
- **Physical:** touch, personal space, and sexual activity with specific people.

Boundaries also cover topics such as religion and money.

**Be mindful of the situation.** Observe how much, and what kind of, information others share and respond in kind. Open your boundaries to people who also trust you with theirs; this reciprocity sets how flexible to be.

**Why practice:** firmer boundaries protect you and your self-esteem; flexible boundaries, where appropriate, let relationships grow.

**Negotiating.** Boundaries are negotiated implicitly (redirecting a conversation, staying quiet about a concern) or explicitly (telling the person directly). Temporary discomfort while a boundary shifts is normal and part of progress.
