Templates for stating thoughts, feelings, and needs assertively, paired with the rights that make doing so legitimate.

**Scripts:**

- **"I think..."** State facts and your perceptions, without judgment or guesses about what the other person thinks or feels. Example: "I think we've been ordering takeout frequently lately and could benefit from cooking at home more often."
- **"I feel..."** Share your emotional experience without attacking, which tends to make people defensive. Avoid "you make me feel". Example: "I feel anxious about our future and would appreciate discussing long-term plans."
- **"I want..."** Make a specific, behavioral request, not a request that someone change their thoughts or feelings. Keep it to one achievable thing. Example: "I want to spend tonight together so we can reconnect."

**Interpersonal rights.** Everyone has the right to:

- express feelings authentically
- disagree respectfully
- decline requests without guilt
- set boundaries
- take time to decide
- be treated with respect

If a conversation stalls despite genuine effort, prioritize self-care and identify concrete next steps toward resolution. Assertiveness takes patient, consistent practice; it doesn't guarantee results but gradually improves relationships.
