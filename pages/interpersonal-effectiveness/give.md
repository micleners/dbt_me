GIVE delivers an important message while protecting the relationship: asking for something, declining, or expressing yourself without alienating the other person. It fits friendships, partners, family, and work situations that call for warmth.

- **G, Be Gentle.** No attacks, threats, or blame. Calm tone, relaxed body language. Gentle isn't passive; it means not escalating needlessly. Skip sarcasm, eye-rolling, shutting down, digging for guilt, or ultimatums. Breathe before responding if tension rises.
- **I, Act Interested.** Show real care for their perspective, even in disagreement. Listen without interrupting, use open body language, nod, ask questions, and stay present instead of rehearsing your rebuttal.
- **V, Validate.** Acknowledge their feelings even if you disapprove of their actions ("That must have been frustrating"). Feeling understood lowers defensiveness.
- **E, Use an Easy manner.** Stay approachable and warm where you can. Use humor appropriately; relax your face, voice, and posture. Casual phrasing ("Hey, can we chat for a second?") keeps people receptive.

**Notes from the source FAQ:** GIVE is respectful delivery of the truth, not sugarcoating. It works in text and email too: no accusations, ALL CAPS, or sarcasm. Feeling fake at first is normal. You can validate without agreeing; use wise mind to see both sides. To stay present, practice Observe and Describe from the Mindfulness module.
