Stand up for yourself without hurting others or losing self-respect, and without excessive apologies or over-explaining.

- **F, Fair.** Respect yourself and the other person. Stay honest and calm, without exaggerating or manipulating. Watch your tone, body language, and words; avoid blame and assumptions.
- **A, No Apologies.** Don't apologize for having needs, setting boundaries, holding opinions, or existing. Drop self-deprecating openers like "I'm sorry to bother you" or "I hate to ask" and say it directly: "I need to set a boundary here."
- **S, Stick to Your Values.** Put long-term integrity ahead of short-term comfort. Under pressure, reconnect with what matters most and ask whether you're acting from fear or integrity.
- **T, Truthful.** Be direct and honest without cruelty or manipulation; no exaggeration, no dishonesty.

**Notes from the source FAQ:** Guilt about holding your values is common in people-pleasers; start small and acknowledge each honest, respectful exchange. You can't control their reaction, only your clarity. To separate honesty from reactivity, pause with STOP and then use wise mind. If you're unsure how to be honest gently, try GIVE. If you don't know your values, see Life Worth Living. For guilt or shame after a mistake, see Radical Acceptance.
