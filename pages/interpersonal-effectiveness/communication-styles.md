Understanding how you typically communicate, and its effect on your relationships, is a core part of interpersonal effectiveness.

**The four styles:**

- **Assertive.** Says thoughts and feelings clearly while respecting both your needs and the other person's. Listens, stays honest in disagreement, stays respectful.
- **Passive.** Holds back thoughts and feelings, often from fear of rejection, criticism, or anger. Can look agreeable while feeling resentful or unheard.
- **Aggressive.** Puts your own needs first with no regard for others. Can be controlling or verbally hostile, dominates, and dismisses other views.
- **Passive-aggressive.** Expresses needs indirectly through sarcasm, the silent treatment, or subtle criticism, leaving others confused or feeling manipulated.

**Self-assessment:** reflect on how you usually respond across situations: whose needs you put first (yours, theirs, or both) and how comfortable you are voicing feelings and standing up for yourself.

Most people use different styles in different situations. Recognizing your patterns comes first; the goal is an assertive style.
