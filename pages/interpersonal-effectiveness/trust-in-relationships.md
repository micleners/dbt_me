Healthy relationships need you to be trustworthy and to surround yourself with dependable people. Past hurts can damage both.

1. **Be honest.** Assess how honest you usually are and whether you trust those around you with your real thoughts and feelings, especially uncomfortable ones. First check whether the person has earned your trust before sharing something vulnerable. Pick a recent situation where honesty was hard and practice wording what you would say despite the discomfort.
2. **Be understanding and respectful.** Help people feel validated, appreciated, and heard by reflecting their view and feelings without judgment. People struggle to be honest when they expect criticism. Everyone acts from reasons and unmet needs; you needn't approve of their choices, but treat them with respect and openness. Reflect on a recent situation where you communicated this way.
3. **Be consistent.** Stability over time builds mutual trust; unpredictable behavior and decisions erode it. Identify recent situations where consistency mattered or would have helped.

See the source page for an example for each step.
