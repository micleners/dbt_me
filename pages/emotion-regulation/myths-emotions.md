Society sends mixed messages about emotions. This exercise identifies and challenges common myths.

**Eight myths and their opposing views:**

1. Showing feelings is weakness. *Opposing view:* expressing emotion is normal and a healthy response to life.
2. Emotions are useless. *Opposing view:* they give fast feedback about your attitudes and serve real functions.
3. Feelings are always true. *Opposing view:* emotions can linger and distort reality; let them inform decisions, not make them alone.
4. Acting on urges is sound decision-making. *Opposing view:* intense emotion can drive harmful behavior; coping skills lead to better results.
5. Changing emotions is inauthentic. *Opposing view:* accepting emotions brings change naturally, without forced suppression.
6. Everyone should feel the same in the same situation. *Opposing view:* emotional responses vary, and that's normal.
7. Drama makes life richer. *Opposing view:* healthy emotional awareness gives depth without destructive impulses.
8. Impulsivity makes you productive. *Opposing view:* respectful assertiveness and proactive planning work better long-term.

**Practice:**

1. Review the myths and note which resonate.
2. Rate each 1–10 for how much it shows up in your thinking.
3. For your top three, examine how they affect your behavior and imagine different outcomes.
