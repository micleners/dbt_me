Recognizing problem situations lets you prepare in advance and gives you a sense of control over emotionally overwhelming circumstances.

1. **Behavior analysis.** Identify the triggering event, the primary emotion, and the accompanying thoughts and feelings.
2. **What can I change?** Assess which parts are modifiable. You often can't change the external event, but you can work on your thoughts and how you talk to yourself during it. Pick two aspects to address, favoring what you control.
3. **Brainstorm alternatives.** Generate different thoughts or behaviors: reframe cognitive distortions, challenge generalizations, or change your response (for example, self-compassionate statements or assertive boundary-setting).
4. **Put the solution into action** when the situation comes up again. Consistent practice turns this into a learnable skill.
